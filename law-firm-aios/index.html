Sound familiar?

AI in the firm, without the safety net

Most lawyers use generative AI at work now. Very few firms have built it into how they work, trained their people or written a policy they can follow.

Everyone doing it their own way

Different tools, different prompts, and client details pasted into who knows what.

Accountable for all of it

The SRA has been clear that the firm answers for AI-assisted work, whoever or whatever drafted it.

The non-billable hours

File notes, chasing, first drafts and supervision still fill the week.

In LexisNexis research from August 2025, 61% of UK legal professionals said they used generative AI at work, but only 17% said it was built into how their firm operates. Only 28% had an AI policy that was easy to follow, and 24% said they had been given the right training.

In August 2026 the SRA published a warning notice on the misuse of AI. Firms stay accountable for all their work, AI-assisted or not, and free or consumer AI tools may not give the safeguards client confidentiality and privilege need. Its updated supervision guidance asks firms to show how authorised solicitors supervise others’ work, and that AI output gets proper human review.

A Law Firm AI Operating System closes that gap. It gives everyone the same safe, supervised way of using AI, set up for your firm’s precedents, departments and clients, and it keeps the record that shows how the work was checked.

Common questions

What partners usually ask

Does it give legal advice?

No. It prepares, drafts, summarises and flags. A solicitor reviews the work and decides what goes to the client, which is what the SRA expects. Where the system isn’t sure of something, it is set up to flag it rather than guess.

What about client confidentiality and privilege?

The system runs on a business Claude plan with a data processing agreement, and Anthropic doesn’t train its models on your inputs or outputs. Client files stay in your own SharePoint and systems, and reach the AI only through approved connections when someone runs a task. This is very different from staff pasting client details into a free AI tool, which is the risk the SRA warned about.

Each person’s AI works with that person’s own access, so it sees only what they can already see, and ethical walls still hold. In discovery we agree what the AI may and may not see, and write that into your AI policy.

Where is our data held?

Claude’s business plans store data in the US, under the data processing agreement. For most firms the requirement is a lawful international transfer under UK GDPR rather than UK-only storage, but some insurers and clients do insist on UK hosting. If yours do, tell us early and we’ll set out the options, including building on Microsoft Copilot instead.

How does this fit with the SRA’s expectations on AI and supervision?

The SRA’s position is that firms stay accountable for AI-assisted work, that AI output needs proper human review by an authorised person, and that firms should be able to evidence how they supervise. The system is built around that: solicitors approve what goes out, AI-assisted work is logged with who approved it, and a sample of files is prepared for review each week.

We help you put an AI policy and staff guidance in place as part of the set-up. This is practical guidance on how the system works, not legal or regulatory advice, and your COLP stays responsible for your firm’s compliance.

We use Microsoft 365. Why Claude rather than Copilot?

Claude works inside Microsoft 365. It connects to Outlook, SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams, and has add-ins for Word and Excel, so your people keep working where they already do. Several of the best-known legal AI products are built on Claude or connect to it, and Microsoft itself now offers Claude models within Copilot.

If your firm has already standardised on Copilot, or needs UK-only data storage, we can build on Copilot instead. It takes a little more set-up, and we’ll be clear about any difference in what it can do.

Our case management system already has AI features. Why add this?

Those features work inside one system. A Law Firm AI Operating System works across the firm: email, documents, case management, the phone and your precedents, and it knows how your firm does things. Your case management system stays the record of matters, time and client money, and the AI reads from it where it can.

Do we need a legal AI product such as Lexis+ AI or Harvey as well?

Not to start. Most of the time a high street firm loses goes on the work around legal work: notes, drafts from precedents, chasing and supervision. That is what this system does. For authoritative legal research, connect a subscription you already have, such as Practical Law, Lexis or CoCounsel. Without one, the system flags research questions for a solicitor rather than answering them.

Do we need our precedents in order first?

No. If they are well organised, the system connects to them and follows your filing rules. If they are scattered across personal drives and old matters, the set-up includes agreeing a structure and gathering the current versions, and the AI does much of that work. Most firms have been meaning to do it for years.

Could we build it ourselves?

Yes. Doing it well takes well over 100 hours, plus the know-how to make it complete, safe and easy to keep working. We have a proven, repeatable method, so your people spend their time on the knowledge only they have, and the firm gets the benefit in weeks.

We’re a small firm. Is this for us?

It suits firms from around ten people upwards, and a smaller start works well: a safe set-up and AI policy, plus one workflow such as attendance notes or probate letters. You see the result before deciding on more.

How will we know it has worked?

Before launch we time two or three tasks your fee earners do often, such as an attendance note or a first-draft letter. We time them again a few weeks after launch. Alongside that you can see how many people use it each week and, over time, the effect on unbilled time and lock-up.

The morning brief

What it is

A short briefing waiting for each fee earner before the day starts, put together overnight from their email, diary, matters and tasks.

What it does for you

  • The day starts on client work, not on the inbox.
  • Deadlines and key dates are flagged before they become a problem.
  • Routine replies are already drafted, ready to check and send.

How it works

A scheduled task runs early each morning. It reads the fee earner’s inbox and diary, checks key dates and stalled files on their matters, and pulls in the task list. It prepares a short brief for each appointment and drafts replies to routine emails. It uses the firm’s procedures to decide what matters most, and shows the result as a simple dashboard.
