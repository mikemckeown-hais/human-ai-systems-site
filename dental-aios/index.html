A phone that never stops, and numbers nobody quite trusts

Most practices aren’t short of patients who want to come in. They’re short of hours at the front desk, and of a clear view of what’s happening.

Calls lost after six

A front desk stretched thin

Reports that take an afternoon

Recruiting and keeping staff is the top concern for most UK practices. Dentist vacancy rates sit in the mid to high teens, and rises in employer National Insurance and the minimum wage have pushed up the cost of every front desk hour.

At the same time, more of a practice’s income now depends on private work, where one missed evening enquiry can be a course of treatment worth thousands. And price transparency matters more: the Competition and Markets Authority is studying private dentistry pricing, with its final report due in early 2027.

Many practices have tried an AI receptionist. They answer the phone, but they know little about the practice behind it, and they don’t touch the rest of the admin. A Dental AI Operating System starts from the practice’s own knowledge and works across the whole day.

The AI front desk

A front desk that knows your practice

Plenty of AI receptionists can answer a phone. This one answers from your practice’s own knowledge, and the system behind it reviews every call overnight, so it gets better each week.

What it does

Answers in a voice you choose, says it’s AI, answers questions from your own price list and policies, books routine appointments and takes the details of every new enquiry.

What it never does

It never gives clinical advice, never diagnoses and never decides what’s urgent on its own judgement. It follows rules your dentists sign off, and hands to a person when in doubt.

When it’s urgent

Heavy bleeding, trauma or a knocked-out tooth go straight to the on-call dentist, by the route your practice uses, or to NHS 111, with a summary waiting in the morning report.

How you get one

Set up with your practice in a few weeks

The same six stages we use for any Business AI Operating System, adapted for a dental practice. Reports go live first, and the phones come last.

  1. DiscoveryWe learn how your practice works
  2. BuildYour knowledge, reports and dental skills
  3. ImplementReports live first
  4. TrainYour team learns by doing, role by role
  5. ExtendMessages, then the phone
  6. SupportMonthly reviews, and it supports itself

1 · Discovery

We learn how your practice works: the team, the practice software, the phones, which appointments can be booked without a person, how emergencies are handled and where the time goes. We time two or three everyday tasks and count missed calls, so there’s a baseline to measure against.

2 · Build

We build the knowledge base from your price list, policies and templates, the morning report and practice dashboard, and the first dental skills, such as enquiry replies and treatment plan letters. You review the drafts and tell us what doesn’t sound like you.

3 · Implement

We package it as a plugin, install it, and connect email, files and the practice software as they’re approved. Reports go live first, because they take nothing over. A data protection impact assessment and processing agreement are in place before any patient information is involved.

4 · Train

Short, hands-on sessions on real work, role by role: reception, treatment coordinators, clinicians and management. Everyone leaves using it, and one person is named to own it day to day.

5 · Extend When you’re ready

Messaging next: web chat, text and WhatsApp. Then the AI front desk on the phone, out of hours first, with the emergency rules and what it may and may not say signed off by your dentists before any patient hears it.

6 · Support

Monthly reviews of calls and messages, so the system improves from real conversations. It also has its own support skill, so it helps the team use it and brings new starters up to speed.

We measure as we go: missed calls, enquiries answered, plans followed up and the time the team gets back.

Common questions

What practice owners usually ask

Is this just another AI receptionist?

No. An AI receptionist answers the phone. A Dental AI Operating System starts with the practice’s own knowledge and works across the whole day: reports, letters, enquiries, follow-up and, when you’re ready, the phones. The phone agent answers from what the practice knows, and the system reviews its calls each night so it improves.

Will it give patients clinical advice?

No, never. It books, answers practical questions from your own price list and policies, and passes anything clinical or urgent to a person, following rules your dentists sign off. Drafts such as treatment plan letters are always checked by the dentist. Tools that support administration for a clinician to check are treated very differently from tools that diagnose, and we keep it firmly on the administration side.

What about patient data and GDPR?

Health information is special category data, so it’s handled with care from the start. Clinical records stay in your practice software. The system runs on a business AI plan with a data processing agreement, and each person’s AI only sees what they already can. Before any patient information is involved, we put in place a data protection impact assessment, a processing agreement and an updated privacy notice that covers AI and call recording.

Will patients know they’re talking to AI?

Yes. The AI front desk says so at the start of every call and chat. That’s the right thing to do, it’s what consumer law and the emerging guidance for health regulators expect, and in our experience patients are fine with it when the answer is quick and accurate.

Which practice software does it work with?

Reports and follow-up lists can work with any system you can export from. Live booking connects through each supplier’s approved integration routes, which differ between Dentally, Software of Excellence, EXACT, R4 and others. We confirm what’s possible with yours in discovery, before you commit to anything.

Will it replace our receptionists?

It takes on the repetitive part of the job: the evening calls, the routine questions, the chasing and the letters. Most practices use that time for the patients in front of them, for treatment coordination, or to cover the hours they can’t recruit for. Some choose to run with a smaller front desk. That’s your decision, and the system works either way.

We’re an NHS or mixed practice. Does it still fit?

Yes. Urgent care matters more than ever for NHS contracts in England, so how urgent calls are handled on the phone is part of the design, and recall messaging follows your recall intervals. Private, implant and cosmetic work is where a missed enquiry costs most, but the admin it takes on is the same in any practice.

What does it cost?

A one-off set-up, then a monthly fee for the system, support and, if you add it, the AI front desk. It depends on the size of the practice and which steps you take, so we’ll give you a clear figure after a short conversation. We’d rather it paid for itself in patients booked than compete on being cheapest.

How will we know it has worked?

Before launch we count missed calls and time two or three everyday tasks, such as writing a treatment plan letter or putting the monthly figures together. We measure again a few weeks after launch, alongside enquiries answered, plans followed up and new patients booked.

The morning report

What it is

A short report waiting for the practice manager and owner before the practice opens, put together overnight from the calls, messages, diary and treatment plans.

What it does for you

  • The day starts on what matters, not on the voicemail.
  • Overnight emergencies and new patients are at the top, with what was done about them.
  • Gaps in the diary and plans to chase are listed every day, so nothing slips.

How it works

A scheduled task runs overnight. It reads the inbox, the overnight call and chat summaries, today’s diary and, once connected, the practice software. It uses what the knowledge base knows about your practice to decide what matters most, and shows the result as a simple dashboard. It’s usually the first thing a practice switches on, because it changes nothing for patients.

Treatment plan letters

What it is

A clear letter for the patient, drafted from the dentist’s voice note or notes, with each option explained in plain English and the right fees and finance.

What it does for you

  • Patients have their plan the same day, while they’re still thinking about it.
  • Fees and finance always match the current price list.
  • Dentists spend minutes checking rather than an evening writing.

How it works

The dentist records a short voice note or adds notes after the consultation. The AI drafts the letter from the practice’s templates, explains each option simply, adds fees and finance from the price list, and sets a follow-up reminder for the treatment coordinator. The dentist checks and signs off every letter. The clinical decisions are always theirs.

Enquiry replies

What it is

Replies to new patient and treatment enquiries from email, web forms and chat, drafted from what the practice knows, with times to book.

What it does for you

  • New patients hear back while they’re still choosing a practice.
  • Every reply is accurate on fees, finance and what happens next.

The AI reads each enquiry, works out what the person wants, and drafts a reply in the practice’s tone with the right next step: a consultation time, a price, or a call back from a person. It knows which appointments can be offered without a person and which need one. Reception checks and sends, until the practice is happy for some replies to go on their own.

The AI front desk

What it is

An AI that answers the practice phone and chat when the team isn’t there, or when the line is busy, from what the practice knows.

What it does for you

  • No call goes to voicemail.
  • Routine appointments are booked, and new patients are captured.
  • Emergencies reach the on-call dentist, and the rest is in the morning report.

How it works

Calls divert to the AI front desk, provided with a specialist voice partner, so the practice number doesn’t change. It says it’s AI, answers from the practice’s price list and policies, books routine appointments where the practice software allows, and follows the emergency rules your dentists sign off: urgent cases go to the on-call dentist or NHS 111. It never gives clinical advice. Every call is summarised for the morning, and the system reviews the calls each night to improve the answers.
