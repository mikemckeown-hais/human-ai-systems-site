Common questions

What sales leaders usually ask

Is this more software we have to buy?

No. It runs on the AI platform your company already pays for, such as Microsoft Copilot, Claude or ChatGPT, and connects to the systems you already have. What you pay for is setting it up for your sales team, training them and, if you want it, ongoing support.

Our IT team looks after AI. Isn’t this their job?

IT looks after the tool: which AI platform you use, security, access and which connections are approved. We work with them and within those decisions, using the AI they have already approved. If you haven’t chosen a platform yet, we can suggest one and help your IT team through their assessment.

Setting the tool up for sales is a different job. Think of Excel. IT provides it, but the finance team builds the models, the pivot tables and the macros, because only finance knows how the numbers need to work. A Sales AI Operating System is the sales team’s version of that, and it needs people who know how sales works. One governed setup for the whole team is also easier for IT to approve and support than everyone building their own.

Who can see what? Is our data secure?

Each person’s AI works with that person’s own access, nothing more. A rep’s AI sees their own email and calendar, only the shared files and folders they can already open, and only the CRM records they can see, changing only the fields their CRM permissions allow them to change.

The same applies to the sales leader. Their AI sees what they can see, which usually means their own email and not their team’s, unless company policy already gives them wider access. Setting up the Sales AI Operating System doesn’t grant anyone new access.

Customer data stays in your own systems and reaches the AI only through your approved connections when someone runs a task, under your AI platform’s data terms. If we need temporary access to install it for you, that is covered by a data processing agreement and a confidentiality agreement.

What can we do with LinkedIn and Sales Navigator?

LinkedIn is central to most sales teams, and its rules are often misunderstood. In short:

Fine to do. Schedule and publish posts, and see views and engagement, through tools that use LinkedIn’s official interfaces. Use Sales Navigator as normal, including its official CRM sync, which links the leads and accounts your reps save to your CRM; the AI then works with them from the CRM. When preparing for a meeting, a rep can give the AI what they have read on someone’s profile, and it combines that with your CRM, emails and web research.

Technically possible, but against LinkedIn’s terms, so risky. Anything that automates LinkedIn itself: AI agents that control a browser to visit profiles or send messages, browser extensions that copy profiles or Sales Navigator lists, and third-party tools that log in as you to send connection requests and messages, even when they connect neatly to your AI. LinkedIn’s User Agreement prohibits bots, scrapers and automation add-ons, and accounts using them risk being restricted. Sales Navigator has no native export to a spreadsheet, which is why the CRM sync is the safe route.

How we set it up. LinkedIn is used the way LinkedIn allows, by the rep, and the automation happens in your own systems. LinkedIn changes its features and rules from time to time, so we check the current position during set-up. This is practical guidance, not legal advice.

Do we need a well-organised knowledge base first?

No. There are usually two situations, and both work.

Your information is already well organised. Things live in a clear structure in your shared drives, people know where to find them and where to put them. The Sales AI Operating System connects to what you have, learns and indexes it, and follows your filing rules, much as a good new employee would.

Your information is scattered. Proposals sit in individual reps’ own drives, versions multiply, and nobody is sure which battlecard is current. Here the set-up includes an agreed structure for a shared sales workspace and moving the right material into it, and the AI does much of that work itself. That solves a problem most teams have put off for years because nobody had the time. It pays back quickly, for the AI and for the people.

The same is true of the CRM. If records are incomplete or inconsistent, the AI can help clean them up and keep them that way.

We already have a CRM and a call recording tool. Why add this?

It builds on them rather than replacing them. Your CRM and call recording tools show you what is happening in the pipeline. This does the rep’s preparation, follow-up and drafting work, using the same information, and keeps the CRM more up to date as a result.

Could we build it ourselves?

Yes. Doing it well takes well over 100 hours, plus the know-how to make it complete and keep it working. We have a proven, repeatable method, so your people spend their time on the knowledge only they have, and the team gets the benefit in weeks.

Will the reps use it?

That is what the pilot and the launch are for. Four or five reps test it first and become its champions. The launch is hands-on, on people’s own accounts, with skills for each role. Then we track how many people use it each week, and what is getting used.

How will we know it has worked?

Before launch we time two or three tasks your reps do often, such as meeting preparation or a first-draft proposal. We time them again a few weeks after launch. Alongside that you can see adoption across the team, proposal turnaround and, over time, win rates.

The morning brief

What it is

A short briefing waiting for each rep before the day starts, put together overnight from their email, calendar, CRM and tasks.

What it does for you

  • The day starts on the things that move deals forward, not on the inbox.
  • Replies that need sending are already drafted in your voice, ready to check and send.
  • Nothing slips. Quiet deals, overdue follow-ups and tasks due are flagged every day.

How it works

A scheduled task runs overnight. It reads the rep’s inbox and calendar, checks the CRM for deals that have gone quiet or have close dates coming up, and pulls in the task list. It prepares a brief for each meeting and drafts a reply to each important email. It uses your sales method from the knowledge base to decide what matters most, and shows the result as a simple dashboard.
