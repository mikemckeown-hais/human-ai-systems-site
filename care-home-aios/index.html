Safe and governed

Built the way the care sector’s AI guidance asks

The Department of Health and Social Care’s guidance on using AI in adult social care asks providers for an AI policy, careful data handling, transparency and human review of AI output. A Care Home AI Operating System is set up that way from the start.

People decide

The AI prepares, checks, chases and drafts. It never scores or rejects a candidate, and never makes a care decision.

Your data, your systems

Everything stays in your own Microsoft 365 or Google storage, on a business plan with a data processing agreement.

Resident data, carefully

Resident records stay in your care record, and reach the AI only through an approved route, with a DPIA in place.

A policy and a trail

Your AI policy is agreed first, staff are trained on it, and every action is logged.

Staff and operational data first. Recruitment, training, rotas, audits and reporting use information about your staff and your services. That is where most providers start, and it can go live quickly with the right agreements in place.

Resident information through your care record. Resident records are special category health data, often about adults who may lack capacity. They stay in your care record. The AI reads only what a task needs, through a regular export or an approved connection, once a DPIA, a data agreement and your lawful basis are in place. Where you need UK or EU data handling, we take that into account when choosing the route, including the AI built into an assured care record.

Human review, always. Anything the AI drafts about a person, whether a care plan review, a family update or a reply to a candidate, is checked by a member of staff before it is used or sent. Statutory notifications are prepared for a person to submit.

Your AI policy. If you don’t have one yet, we help you write it, including the rule that matters most: no resident information in free or personal AI tools. We advise and design. You stay the data controller and the accountable provider, and formal legal or DPO sign-off stays with you.

The same six stages we use for any Business AI Operating System, adapted for care. We usually start with one home and one or two workflows, then roll out across the group.

Governance comes first. Your AI policy, the DPIA and the data rules are agreed in discovery, before anything goes live, so your DPO and your directors are comfortable from day one.

1 · Discovery

We learn how your homes work: the services, the people, the paperwork that fills the week and the systems you use. We spend time with your directors, a registered manager or two and whoever looks after your IT, review your policies and templates, and agree the first workflows. We agree your AI policy, the DPIA and the data rules at the same time, and time two or three everyday tasks so there is a baseline to measure against.

2 · Build

We build your Care Home AI Operating System on the AI platform you already use: the knowledge base from your policies and templates, the standard skills, care skills for the workflows you chose, and dashboards such as the morning brief and recruitment tracker. You try the skills on real work, with test data first.

3 · Implement

One home uses it on live work first. We test each workflow with your staff, connect email, calendar and files as they are approved, fix what gets in the way, then switch on the schedules and roll out to the other homes.

4 · Train

Managers and the office team learn by doing, on their own homes’ work, in sessions of an hour or so once a week for a few weeks, on site or online. Training covers your AI policy and the data guardrails as well as the skills.

5 · Extend Optional

When you’re ready, we take it further: your care record, rota and training systems connected, front-of-house workflows such as activity digests and family updates, and an AI phone agent for out-of-hours enquiries.

6 · Support

When the programme ends, the system supports itself and your people. It has its own support skill, so it helps managers use it, brings new staff on board and helps fix issues. Optional support packages are there if you want us on hand.

We measure as we go: the same everyday tasks timed again after launch, alongside how many people use it each week.

Common questions

What care providers usually ask

Is this more software we have to buy?

No. It runs on the AI platform your organisation already pays for, such as Microsoft Copilot, Claude or ChatGPT, and connects to the systems you already have. What you pay for is setting it up for your homes, training your managers and, if you want it, ongoing support.

Do our carers need an AI licence each?

No. Carers keep recording in your care record, on the handsets they already use. The system runs for managers and the office team, and reads what it needs from the care record through an approved route. That keeps the cost down and asks nothing new of the people delivering care.

Is it safe with resident information?

Resident records stay in your care record. Most providers start in the back office, on staff and operational information. When you want the AI to work with resident information, it does so only through an approved route, once there is a DPIA, a data agreement and a clear lawful basis, and a member of staff reviews everything it produces. We take account of where the data is processed when choosing the route.

What does CQC think about AI?

CQC looks at whether care is safe and well led, and that includes how you manage information and technology. The Department of Health and Social Care has published guidance on using AI in adult social care: have an AI policy, check where data is processed, keep personal information out of free tools, tell people how their data is used, and always have a person review AI output. A Care Home AI Operating System is designed around those points, and it makes your evidence easier to find.

Will the AI make decisions about candidates or residents?

No. It prepares, checks, chases, drafts and flags. People interview, decide, approve and send. In recruitment it never scores, ranks or rejects anyone. In care it never changes a care plan or sends anything to a family on its own.

Our care record already has AI features. Why add this?

Use them. Care record AI, such as voice notes and care plan suggestions, helps on the floor inside that one system. A Care Home AI Operating System works across the business: recruitment, training, rotas, audits and reporting, joining up the care record with everything else. The two work well together.

We bought software before and nobody used it. Why would this be different?

Because it asks people to change very little. Managers keep working in email and the tools they know, and carers keep using the care record. The AI fits around how your homes already run. We start with one home, train people on their own work, and track how many people use it each week, so you can see whether it is working.

Do we need our documents well organised first?

No. If your policies, templates and staff files are well organised, the system connects to what you have. If they are spread across shared drives, inboxes and filing cabinets, the set-up includes an agreed structure and moving the right material into it, and the AI does much of that work itself.

Could we build it ourselves?

Yes. Doing it well takes well over 100 hours, plus the know-how to get the governance right in a regulated sector. We have a proven, repeatable method, so your managers spend their time on the knowledge only they have, and the benefit arrives in weeks.

How will we know it has worked?

Before launch we time two or three tasks your managers do often, such as checking a new starter’s file or preparing the monthly quality review. We time them again a few weeks after launch. Alongside that you can see adoption across your homes, time to hire and, over time, the state of your CQC evidence.

The morning brief

What it is

A short briefing waiting for each manager before the shift starts, put together from their email, calendar, the rota, the recruitment tracker and the training records.

What it does for you

  • The day starts on what matters, not on the inbox.
  • Staffing gaps are flagged early, with who to try first.
  • Nothing slips: expiring training, overdue supervisions and open actions are listed every day.

How it works

A scheduled task runs early each morning. It reads the manager’s inbox and calendar, checks the rota for gaps, looks at new applications and checks in progress, and pulls in the open actions. It drafts replies to emails that need one, and shows the result as a simple dashboard on the desktop and the phone.

Meeting and supervision write-ups

What it is

A written record of the daily meeting, a supervision, a team meeting or an investigation interview, made from a voice note, a recording or rough notes.

What it does for you

  • Every action has an owner and a date, and none get lost.
  • Supervision records are complete and filed the same day.
  • Requests and follow-ups that came out of the meeting are drafted ready to send.

How it works

The manager records a short voice note on their phone, or uploads a recording or notes. The AI writes it up in your template, files it by home and by person, adds the actions to the tracker and drafts any emails. The manager checks it before anything is sent.

Recruitment and onboarding

What it is

The candidate journey run in the background: applications checked, gaps explained, references and checks chased, the pre-employment documents sent and filed, and every step logged.

What it does for you

  • Good candidates hear back in minutes, not days, so fewer go elsewhere.
  • Every file is complete before a start date, with the Regulation 19 checks recorded.
  • Managers spend their time interviewing, not chasing.

How it works

Applications arrive by email or form. The AI checks each one for completeness, asks the candidate to fill gaps or explain breaks in their employment history, requests references from the right employers and chases them, and tracks DBS and right to work. A dashboard shows each candidate’s checks, and managers make decisions by email or on the dashboard. The AI never scores, ranks or rejects a candidate. People decide.

CQC readiness

What it is

A live evidence library for each home, filed under the five key questions CQC asks, Safe, Effective, Caring, Responsive and Well-led, with the gaps and actions in view.

What it does for you

  • Evidence builds up as the work gets done, not in a rush when the call comes.
  • Gaps are visible early, while there’s time to fix them.
  • Your self-assessment and action plans stay current.

How it works

Audits, training records, supervisions, meeting notes and action plans are filed as the other skills produce them. Each month a scheduled task maps them to the key questions, lists what’s missing or late, and drafts updates to the home’s self-assessment for the manager to edit.
